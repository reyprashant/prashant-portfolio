_By: That One Guy Who Can't Say No to Leadership Roles. Also: That One Guy Who Forgets He's a Full-Time Student Too._

## The beginning of my slightly chaotic journey

Let me take you on a _slightly caffeinated, occasionally chaotic_, but deeply fulfilling journey through my life in clubs and communities. A place where introverts become leaders.

I was looking to contribute, to lead, to build. Somewhere along the way, _I realised I was the one being built._

I had one goal: _do cool stuff and maybe get a free t-shirt._

They say life is a journey.

**Mine?** _More like a browser with 47 tabs open and zero chill._

I started like everyone does: confused, overenthusiastic, and with zero idea what an agenda is. I said yes to one meeting. That meeting turned into a position. That position turned into "Hey, can you just lead this whole thing?" And suddenly I was juggling more roles than a Marvel multiverse.

## The club diaries: where it all got real

### Leo Club of Pokhara Abhiyan

This is where I wore a formal shirt on purpose and addressed a crowd without mentally blacking out for the first time.

I was an _introvert_ back then, giving my first training session to tiny tots while battling serious _stage fright_. I had no idea how I would make it through ten whole minutes. Ironically, my co-trainer ended up blaming me _for taking too much time._

I showed up as a curious volunteer and trainer, and somehow left as President. _(Honestly, I blinked and the sash was already on me.)_

Leo Club taught me to serve with heart and lead with purpose, while also mastering the art of saying "Namaste".

### What I learned

- Service brings peace. _And occasional chaos._
- Service is satisfying. _Until you are carrying 50 chairs up the stairs and standing until the programme ends._
- Public speaking becomes easier _(not easier at all, still learning, just flexing for now)_ after you embarrass yourself four times in a row.

Thanks to Leo, I discovered the magical art of leading with purpose, and the even harder magic of saying "no" to _"just one more responsibility"._

### LA GRANDEE TechTalk

_This one was chaotic good._

I gave pep talks that felt like TED Talks and met some incredible young minds along the way. Somehow everyone thought I had it all under control.

_(Spoiler: I did not.)_

### HULT Prize Campus Director: a beautiful disaster

Ever heard the phrase "fake it till you make it"?

Yeah. I faked it, gave my 100 percent, and still did not quite make it.

### What I learned

- You cannot win alone.
- Starting something new is hard. Really hard. _"I need five coffees a day"_ hard.
- Teamwork beats solo suffering.
- Failing is okay if you are funny about it later.

### Been around, a lot

I have also been Treasurer, Advisor and more in other organisations. Let's hold those stories for another day.

## Next up: Code for Change, my entry-level chaos

Then came the big one: Code for Change.

I joined as a College Representative. One moment _I'm just helping out_, next moment, bam, I'm the Project Lead.

I was hoping to do "a little bit" and somehow became the Project Lead. _(Again, not flexing at all. I swear these promotions happen faster than I can update my LinkedIn.)_

CFC became my part-time job, therapy session and second home, all rolled into one.

I learned how to actually build things, manage people and navigate the mystical art of team dynamics without losing my mind. _(Without entirely losing my mind.)_

This tenure at CFC has been full of real impact, from technical events to community building. I have met people who challenge me, inspire me, and remind me to send the Google Form on time.

### If Leo taught me to speak, CFC taught me to act, build and execute

- To move beyond ideas and into real execution.
- To build something that lasts.
- To lead without losing yourself. _(Okay, fine. Maybe a little of myself. Like my weekends.)_

This community taught me things I did not even know I needed to learn. Like:

- How to lead without losing your mind _(still working on it)_.
- How to be the person who asks _"Can we hop on a quick call?"_ at 11:47 pm.
- How to follow, how to listen, and how to trust a team that sometimes forgets deadlines but never forgets to show up.
- Actual project management.

I met incredible people, faced terrifying deadlines, and learned the three sacred pillars of community work:

1. If it is not on Google Drive, it does not exist.
2. There is always one person who forgets the task, even if it is you.
3. Everyone loves pizza at meetings but forgets to bring the budget.

Somewhere in between, I have been "also here" at various events: volunteering, organising, cheering, and somehow always being handed the mic for "just a few words".

My go-to phrase? _"I wasn't prepared, but..."_ followed by a five-minute TED Talk.

## In conclusion?

Join a community. Get overwhelmed. Learn stuff. Teach stuff. Forget to eat. Repeat.

And slowly, without realising it, you go from asking **"What do I do?"** to **"How can I help?"**

If you are wondering how I balanced it all: I didn't.

_My life was one giant overlapping Venn diagram of chaos, commitment and community._

But every role, every meeting, every _"Can you handle this?"_ moment made me who I am.

To sum it up?

Join a club.

Lose your peace.

Find your people.

Accidentally become president.

Repeat.
